Cureatr / Case study

From scattered medication records to a tool used across 20+ hospital systems

01 Summary02 Problem03 What we learned04 Key decision05 Design06 Outcome07 Reflection

01 / Summary

Lead UX Designer · 2016–2019

I owned the timeline interaction and alert design, working with a UX researcher, a product manager with nurse-practitioner expertise, and a mobile developer.

02 / Problem

Clinicians had to assemble a patient’s medication history from prescription records, printouts, and the patient’s memory. Before they could make a decision, they had to piece the evidence together.

03 / What we learned

The researcher and I took a working prototype to hospital staff around New York. Clinicians took readily to horizontal scrolling, and most of the staff we met used iOS.

04 / Key decision

Organize medication history as a horizontal timeline so clinicians can read prescriptions, gaps, and overlapping use in time. I learned Origami to test the interaction with a prototype that behaved like the intended product.

For overlapping controlled-substance prescriptions, use a full-screen alert with prescriber, pharmacy, and date range. This was information that needed to interrupt the workflow, rather than sit in a quiet badge.

Editorial note The existing account does not identify anyone who pushed back on the timeline or alert decision.

05 / Design

06 / Outcome

Deployed across 20+ hospital systems. Mather Hospital credited the product with helping staff see medications and pharmacy sources without calling pharmacies, saving time and helping avoid errors.

07 / Reflection

The alert tested well alone, but competed with alerts clinicians already saw throughout the day. I stripped it back to what needed to interrupt. In a clinical workflow, visual emphasis has to earn the attention it takes.
